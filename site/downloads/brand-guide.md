# Church Times Nigeria — digital media brand guide

Proposed direction · Version 0.1 · 5 October 2026

This is a working brand system, not an approved identity or legal clearance. The founder interview supplies the mission. Live Nigerian .net and UK comparison findings are documented in the research report. The requested .com/.org identities and trademark/domain clearance remain unresolved. The publication must not imply a relationship with the UK Church Times.

## 1. Brand foundation

**Mission:** report the church with the rigour of independent journalism and follow people of faith into the wider life of society.

**Purpose:** help readers understand how faith, institutions and everyday decisions affect public life.

**Positioning:** for readers seeking credible, thoughtful coverage of the church and its social impact, Church Times Nigeria brings a Nigerian perspective, reporting experience and a potential print archive. Those assets support a proposition; verified readership and demonstrated reporting will earn its authority.

**Signature:** Faith. People. Public life.

**Editorial expression:** Independent minds. Shared humanity.

**Values translated into practice:** independence → no sale of favourable coverage; accuracy → corroboration and visible corrections; fairness → right of reply; dignity → consent and source protection; stewardship → disciplined costs and transparent commercial labels.

Primary audience hypotheses: church-engaged professionals, ministry leaders, entrepreneurs, diaspora readers and readers interested in faith and public affairs. Validate needs and willingness to pay before assuming a membership market.

## 2. Naming and identity

Use **Church Times Nigeria** on first reference, mastheads, social bios, metadata, commercial documents and email signatures. “Church Times” is acceptable in subsequent context where Nigerian identity is clear. Legal invoices must use the registered entity once confirmed.

The prototype's serif wordmark is a proposed typographic identity, not a registered logo. Keep “NIGERIA” visible beneath it. Use forest on parchment or parchment on forest; no bevels, drop shadows, gradients, cramped spacing or arbitrary colours.

Minimum digital wordmark width: 180px when the Nigeria line is present; check legibility on the actual device. Clear space: at least the height of the capital C on every side in dedicated identity artwork. Editorial website headers may use a tighter layout only if navigation remains separate and legible. Minimum-size and clear-space specifications need review in final vector artwork.

Avatar proposal: a simple CT monogram with a readable Nigerian identifier in the profile name. Do not rely on small text inside a 32px avatar. Commission a final vector identity only after name/trademark review.

## 3. Colour system

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Forest | #173B32 | Primary actions, masthead, dark newsletter surface |
| Parchment | #F6F3EA | Main reading surface and text on forest |
| Ink | #222A25 | Body text and headlines |
| Clay | #A45336 | Restricted accent, section labels and emphasis |
| Sage | #DFE5D8 | Secondary reading surfaces and callouts |
| Muted | #61675E | Secondary text on light surfaces |
| Rule | #D7D8CC | Decorative separators; not a sole focus or input indicator |

Approximate composition: 65% parchment, 20% forest/ink, 10% sage, 5% clay. This is a visual guideline, not a fixed page requirement. Text, status, icons and charts must remain intelligible without colour alone.

Production target: WCAG 2.2 AA. Verify each actual foreground/background combination at its rendered size: 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for qualifying large text and essential UI boundaries. Do not assume every palette combination passes. Avoid clay body text on sage until checked. Use explicit error text, visible focus and correctly labelled controls.

## 4. Typography and reading

Prototype editorial serif: Georgia, Times New Roman, serif. Interface: Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif. These system stacks avoid third-party font requests, licensing surprises and layout shifts. A licensed font family may be introduced after performance, language and accessibility testing.

Production editorial body target: 17–20px, 1.6–1.8 line height, 60–75 characters per line. Homepage summaries are shorter and smaller; production should review mobile text sizes with actual readers. Headlines must communicate a concrete story. Use sentence case. Reserve italic for emphasis and a small number of feature headlines. Avoid long ALL-CAPS headlines; uppercase is suitable for short section labels.

Scale: display 48–80px desktop / 40–56px mobile; section 32–44px; card 24–30px; body 17–20px; UI 12–16px. The design prototype's fine-print treatment needs accessibility review before production.

## 5. Voice and tone

Write with clarity, curiosity and evidence. Address readers as adults. A Christian perspective informs questions; it does not justify unverified claims or substitute for reporting. News, analysis, opinion, devotion and paid promotion need distinct labels.

| Context | Use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| News | Specific claims, named sources, fair replies, verified dates | “Shocking”, “everyone knows”, anonymous certainty |
| Analysis | Explain evidence, assumptions and uncertainty | Presenting interpretation as established fact |
| Opinion | Label the view, name the author and disclose conflicts | Publishing a sponsor's position as newsroom reporting |
| Membership | State the benefit, price, billing and cancellation clearly | Guilt, guaranteed influence or access to editorial decisions |
| Corrections | Identify what changed and when | Silent edits to material factual errors |
| Sensitive topics | Precise, humane language and appropriate context | Sensationalising grief, illness, abuse or religious conflict |

Example: “The report raises questions about how the funds were spent,” followed by evidence and response. Avoid “Shocking revelations rock the entire church!” Never fabricate quotes, interviewees, dates or readership claims to fill a design.

## 6. Editorial architecture

- **Faith & society:** institutions, public policy, education, health and accountability.
- **Church & ministry:** verified church reporting, governance and ministry developments.
- **People & purpose:** profiles and service with independent sourcing.
- **Business & vocation:** faith, work, enterprise and ethical decisions across the economy.
- **Culture & ideas:** books, arts, informed commentary and critical reviews.
- **Archive:** date every historical item prominently and distinguish original text from later corrections.

Publishable article model: title, standfirst, slug, section, content type, author and bio, publication/update dates, source links, body, verified hero image and credit, alt text, rights record, tags, canonical URL, correction history and commercial label where relevant. Opinion additionally needs a viewpoint label. No fabricated “staff” byline or date should stand in for editorial ownership.

Workflow: commission → reporting → evidence review → fact-check → edit → rights/consent check → legal review where warranted → publish → distribute → correct and update. Commercial staff do not approve news treatment or suppress inconvenient reporting.

## 7. Photography and illustration

Prioritise original documentary images, consent, accurate captions and rights. Describe who, where and when. Do not identify vulnerable people without an editorial assessment and appropriate consent. An illustration is labelled “Illustration”; generated imagery must never imply a documentary event. Stock must never imply it depicts a reported subject.

The prototype uses an original, generic city-and-church illustration and CSS artwork. They do not depict a specific church, documented event or original historical cover. The archive mockups are concepts. A production image library should retain creator, licence, consent, date, location, usage limits and alt text.

## 8. Social and newsletter system

Confirm official handles and account ownership first. Do not assume the dormant profiles are recoverable. Use the full Nigerian name in profiles, one verified canonical website link and the same avatar/covers.

Formats: 1080×1350 feed card, 1080×1920 vertical story/video, 1200×630 link preview. Keep essential text in safe areas; add subtitles to video and meaningful alt text where supported. Social posts should drive informed reading, not manufacture outrage.

Three editable, explicitly labelled feed templates are supplied in `public/brand-assets/` and the downloadable ZIP. They are proposed artwork, not published stories or paid partnerships.

Five recommended repeatable templates: verified story lead; explain-the-issue carousel; attributed interview excerpt; archive item with visible date; clearly labelled advertisement. Each retains section, headline, image credit, Nigeria identity and a verified destination link.

Initial cadence hypothesis: one dependable weekly newsletter and three useful social posts per week from published reporting. Scale only when staffing and engagement support it. Measure engagement and return visits, not just impressions.

Newsletter structure: editor's note → three reported stories → one contextual recommendation → one visibly labelled sponsor placement → membership invitation → publisher identity, privacy link and unsubscribe. Build from opt-in records; no scraping or purchased lists. A connection in the founder's address book is not automatic permission for recurring marketing.

## 9. Trust and commercial separation

Adopt named ownership, editorial team, conflicts policy, complaints contact, funding disclosure and a dated corrections register before launch. A sponsor receives advertising deliverables, never a favourable verdict. Historical supporters cannot be displayed as current partners without written permission.

Advertisements and sponsored articles must be recognisable before click and on the destination. Suggested label: “Advertisement” for display placements; “Sponsored content” for paid stories. A sponsored special edition needs an upfront sponsor disclosure and a contractual boundary around editorial approval. Never charge sources for inclusion in independent news.

## 10. Digital product principles

Reading comes first: clear hierarchy, generous spacing, restrained ad density, fast local assets and a dependable mobile experience. Provide keyboard operation, skip link, meaningful headings, form labels, accessible dialogs and visible error states. Use real-time consent and unsubscribe controls in production.

Website launch requires a CMS, editor roles, scheduled backups, real search, verified authors, migration redirects, privacy controls, email service and secure payment handling if membership launches. Use server-side payment verification; never trust a redirect alone as proof of payment. Do not put API keys in the client bundle.

## 11. Brand operations

The editor owns editorial standards; the digital producer owns templates; the commercial lead owns rate cards and sponsorship labels; the founder or delegated publisher approves identity and legal naming. Maintain a shared assets folder, source files, change log and approved examples. Grant minimum required access; keep recovery and offboarding procedures current.

Brand review every quarter: legibility, consistency, reader understanding, accessibility, corrections handling and sponsor-label visibility. Record exceptions and fix repeated mistakes in the template, not just the individual asset.

## 12. Required approval inputs

Confirm legal name and founder attribution; canonical domain and name clearance; final wordmark; verified history; archive rights; approved editorial and corrections policies; official handles; privacy/consent process; team and budget. This guide is useful for review now; these missing inputs prevent a fully approved production brand.
