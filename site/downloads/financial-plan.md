# Church Times Nigeria — sustainable financial operating system

Planning model · Version 0.1 · 5 October 2026

Figures below are transparent scenario inputs, not audited accounts, market benchmarks, promised results or investment projections. The founder's approximately ₦2m per print edition is the only supplied cost observation; its date and inclusions are unknown. All new prices and monthly costs require quotes, paid-pilot validation and accountant review.

## 1. Commercial thesis

Build a small, dependable digital publication before committing to large fixed costs. Convert existing relationships into written, measured commercial agreements. Sell three distinct things: a verified audience, reader-supported journalism, and separately costed publishing/expertise services. Do not sell editorial praise.

The founder should remain an editorial and relationship asset, with a commercial lead responsible for proposals, margins, collections and renewals. Relationships are prospects until a buyer signs and pays; goodwill is not receivable cash. Replace founder-only access with a shared, permission-controlled CRM and documented follow-up.

## 3. Monthly unit economics

Let M be paying members, P the monthly membership price, S paying sponsors, A the sponsor price, J project revenue, and F fixed operating cost. Let dm, ds and dj be variable delivery-cost rates for the three streams.

Gross revenue = M×P + S×A + J.

Variable cost = M×P×dm + S×A×ds + J×dj.

Operating contribution after fixed costs = gross revenue − variable cost − F.

Break-even members = ceiling(max(0, F − S×A×(1−ds) − J×(1−dj)) / (P×(1−dm))). If membership contribution per member is zero while a gap remains, membership cannot close the gap. The calculator handles this case explicitly.

VAT/other taxes, payment fees, bad debt, refunds, founder distributions and investment costs are excluded from these illustrative calculations. Determine which costs are already included in delivery rates and add remaining real costs before approving budgets. Project revenue here is recognised monthly delivery value, not a deposit counted as earned income. For planning, receipts and revenue need separate schedules.

| Monthly scenario | Members × fee | Sponsors × fee | Project revenue | Gross | Variable costs (10% / 20% / 45%) | Fixed costs | Operating result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Conservative | 75 × ₦2,500 | 1 × ₦150,000 | ₦100,000 | ₦437,500 | ₦93,750 | ₦850,000 | **−₦506,250** |
| Baseline hypothesis | 200 × ₦2,500 | 2 × ₦200,000 | ₦300,000 | ₦1,200,000 | ₦265,000 | ₦850,000 | **₦85,000** |
| Growth hypothesis | 400 × ₦2,500 | 3 × ₦250,000 | ₦600,000 | ₦2,350,000 | ₦520,000 | ₦1,100,000 | **₦730,000** |

Baseline member contribution is ₦2,250. With ₦320,000 sponsor contribution and ₦165,000 project contribution, the remaining fixed-cost gap is ₦365,000. Membership breaks even at **163 paying members**, assuming sponsorship and project delivery repeat. Without those streams, it requires **378 members**. These counts are required sales, not forecasts.

Baseline margin is just 7.1% before excluded costs. This is fragile and should not be called financially secure. A 20% loss of member, sponsor and project revenue reduces contribution from ₦935,000 to ₦748,000 and produces a **₦102,000 shortfall** against unchanged fixed costs. Revenue should not be added without reflecting staff capacity and delivery costs.

## 5. Audience and membership acquisition

Use a verified opt-in email list as the first recurring distribution asset. A hypothetical 4% conversion would require 5,000 eligible engaged free subscribers to produce 200 paying members. No such list or conversion rate is established. Model 1%, 2% and 4%, not only the most favourable case; measure trial-to-paid conversion and 30/90-day retention.

Churn matters: 200 members at a hypothetical 5% monthly churn lose 10 members each month and need 10 new paying members just to maintain the base. Monthly net member growth = new paying members − cancellations/failed renewals. Do not confuse cumulative signups with active paid members.

At ₦2,250 contribution per member-month, a proposed three-month cash payback limit implies acquisition spend of at most ₦6,750 per successfully retained member before excluded costs. Do not use this ceiling until retention and payment costs are measured. Begin with permission-based founder introductions, reader referrals and high-value journalism rather than paid acquisition at an unproven conversion rate.

## 6. Print special-edition gate

The reported ₦2m is provisional. Obtain a fresh printer quote and itemised project budget: research, photography, editing, design, rights, print quantity, paper, delivery, sales commission, tax, returned stock and contingency.

Illustrative calculation if ₦2m includes all production/delivery costs: add 15% working contingency = ₦2.3m. A 20% margin on sales requires **₦2.875m revenue** (₦2.3m / 0.8), not merely a 20% markup. If commissions or taxes were excluded, recalculate with them included.

At this target, an 80% cash deposit equals ₦2.3m. A 70% deposit equals ₦2.0125m and would not cover the provisional cost. Therefore the gate is **cleared cash covering the approved committed costs and contingency before committing production**, plus signed agreements and a realistic receivable schedule. A contract or verbal pledge alone does not meet the gate. Deposit percentages should follow actual cash requirements.

No print run proceeds if sponsor pressure compromises journalism, buyers are unverified, costs exceed collected funds, or the team cannot deliver without destabilising the weekly product. Fulfilment obligations on deposits remain liabilities until delivery; do not use client deposits to plug unrelated losses.

## 7. Commercial operating system

**Roles:** founder opens qualified relationships and safeguards mission; commercial lead owns pipeline and collections; editor independently approves editorial boundaries; producer delivers campaigns; bookkeeper reconciles bank receipts and obligations. If one person holds multiple roles, document the separation and have a second person review conflicts and payments.

**CRM fields:** organisation, named contact, permission/source, sector, relevant offer, qualified need, indicative budget, decision maker, next action/date, stage, proposed value, expected close date, delivery cost, signed terms, invoice number, cash received, expiry/renewal date and restrictions. Record history without importing personal addresses indiscriminately.

**Pipeline:** permissioned prospect → discovery → qualified → costed proposal → contract → invoice → cleared deposit → delivery → report → renewal. Weighted pipeline can support planning but cannot be reported as revenue. Review stale proposals weekly. Treat historical supporters as candidates for reintroduction, not current customers.

**Contracts:** scope, placement inventory, dates, creative requirements, disclosure labels, no editorial influence, rights, payment deadlines, cancellation/refund rules, tax treatment, measurement basis and dispute resolution. No unverified audience guarantees.

**Pricing:** price floor = allocated fixed contribution + incremental delivery + acquisition/commission + fees/tax treatment + risk reserve + desired profit. Track campaign and project contribution separately. Cap inventory to protect reading quality.

**Payment policy hypothesis:** sponsorship paid before placements run; services receive a cash-covering deposit and milestone payments; print follows the cash gate. Annual reader billing needs clear benefits and refund/cancellation policies and cannot be recognised entirely as immediate revenue. Use a regulated payment provider, server-side webhook verification, reconciliation and appropriately secured customer records.

## 8. Cash discipline and dashboard

Maintain a rolling **13-week cash forecast**. Opening bank cash + collected receipts − actually due payments = closing cash. Separate tax balances, restricted/client delivery funds and operating cash. Maintain invoice ageing: current, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90 and over 90 days. Report collections separately from invoices.

Initial reserve target hypothesis: three months of ₦850,000 fixed costs = **₦2.55m**, plus separately funded project commitments. A reserve is a target, not available funding. If starting cash is below it, agree a smaller team, staged activity or documented funding; do not assume projected sponsors finance today’s bills.

Weekly dashboard:

| Measure | Definition / decision |
|---|---|
| Cash runway | Unrestricted operating cash ÷ realistic monthly cash burn; inspect the 13-week schedule before commitments. |
| Stream contribution | Receipts/revenue and variable delivery costs tracked separately; stop repeatedly negative offers. |
| Active paying members | Paid, current accounts after cancellations and payment failures. |
| New members / churn | Show both; monitor retention cohorts and reasons for cancellation. |
| Sponsor concentration | Share of collected revenue by sponsor; if one exceeds a proposed 25% review trigger, prepare a replacement plan. |
| Renewals | Eligible renewals, signed renewals and paid renewals tracked separately. |
| Accounts receivable | Age and collection owner; suspend delivery under agreed non-payment terms. |
| Editorial delivery | Promised versus published reported pieces; quality and corrections, not just volume. |
| Audience | Verified subscribers, active readers, clicks and referrals; open rates are directional because privacy features distort them. |
| Project backlog | Signed work, remaining delivery costs and staff capacity. |

Proposed controls: second-person review for spend above ₦100,000; monthly close with bank reconciliation; written disclosure of related-party deals; secure account recovery; no unscheduled founder withdrawals. Thresholds and authority must be approved by the company.

## 9. The first 90 days

**Days 1–30:** confirm legal identity, domain and account ownership, current manager, team, records and consent. Review 12 months of actual accounts and cost one representative edition. Interview at least 12 readers and 8 potential buyers, with consent. Select a manageable cadence. Establish the CRM, rate-card hypotheses, editorial charter and cash forecast. Launch no irreversible migration until backups and URL mapping are tested.

**Days 31–60:** publish one dependable weekly letter and a small number of properly reported pieces the team can support. Test three sponsorship proposals with priced scope; sell one costed publishing pilot only when delivery capacity is clear. Invite paid membership to a verified reader segment. Count paid conversion, not “interested” responses. Pilot five archive editions after rights clearance.

**Days 61–90:** compare actual contribution, collections, member retention, sponsor renewal intent, archive effort and editorial delivery to the model. Agree a go/no-go operating budget based on cash, not launch enthusiasm. Scale only offers with positive contribution, collectable terms and repeat demand. If demand is weak, keep the free editorial product lean and prioritise proven services.

The numbers of interviews and proposals are work targets, not promised sales. If there is no opt-in audience or operating capital, membership revenue is not a prerequisite to a small editorial pilot, but it must be removed from the revenue forecast until demonstrated.

## 10. Production and company decisions still required

Confirm contact-list existence and consent, current website manager and access, legal publisher, exact domains, monthly budget, unrestricted cash, tax obligations, current readership, sponsor demand, payment provider and editorial capacity. Name accountable owners for editor, commercial, digital and accounts. No production sales or recurring charges should start until these decisions and consumer disclosures are approved.
